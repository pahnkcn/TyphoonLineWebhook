# Emerging Drug Trends

Source: [NIDA - Emerging Drug Trends](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/emerging-drug-trends)

## Overview

The drug landscape is constantly evolving with new substances, new patterns of use, and new combinations. NIDA monitors emerging drug trends to inform research, prevention, and public health responses.

## Current Emerging Trends

### 1. Fentanyl and Synthetic Opioids

#### Illicitly Manufactured Fentanyl (IMF)
- Driving the overdose crisis
- Found in heroin, cocaine, methamphetamine, counterfeit pills
- Users often unaware of presence
- 50-100 times more potent than morphine
- 2mg can be lethal

#### Fentanyl Analogs
- Carfentanil (10,000x more potent than morphine)
- Acetylfentanyl
- Butyrfentanyl
- Furanylfentanyl
- Constantly evolving

#### Nitazenes
- Emerging synthetic opioids
- Potency varies
- Increasingly found in drug supplies

### 2. Xylazine ("Tranq" or "Tranq Dope")

#### What is Xylazine?
- Veterinary sedative (not for human use)
- Non-opioid
- Increasingly found in fentanyl supplies
- **Naloxone does NOT reverse xylazine**

#### Effects
- Heavy sedation
- Decreased breathing
- Low blood pressure
- Severe skin wounds and infections

#### Geographic Spread
- Initially in Philadelphia
- Now in multiple states
- Growing concern

#### Health Risks
- Severe skin ulcers and abscesses
- Tissue necrosis
- Amputations in severe cases
- Overdose (especially when combined with fentanyl)

### 3. Tianeptine ("Gas Station Heroin")

#### What is Tianeptine?
- Antidepressant (not approved in US)
- Sold as dietary supplements
- Found in convenience stores and online
- Brand names: ZaZa, Tianna Red

#### Effects
- Opioid-like effects at high doses
- Euphoria
- Pain relief

#### Health Risks
- Addiction
- Withdrawal symptoms
- Dangerous drops in blood pressure and heart rate
- Respiratory depression

### 4. Synthetic Cannabinoids ("Spice," "K2")

#### Overview
- Lab-created chemicals that mimic THC
- Unpredictable effects
- Often much stronger than cannabis
- Constantly changing chemical formulas

#### Health Risks
- Severe agitation
- Anxiety and paranoia
- Psychosis
- Seizures
- Kidney damage
- Death

#### Why Dangerous
- Unknown chemical composition
- Variable potency
- Contaminants
- No quality control

### 5. Designer Benzodiazepines

#### Examples
- Etizolam
- Clonazolam
- Flubromazolam
- Diclazepam

#### Availability
- Purchased online
- Sold as "research chemicals"
- Found in counterfeit pills

#### Risks
- Overdose (especially with opioids)
- Unknown potency
- Addiction
- Dangerous withdrawal

### 6. Novel Psychoactive Substances (NPS)

#### Categories
- Synthetic stimulants
- Synthetic cannabinoids
- Synthetic hallucinogens
- Dissociative drugs
- Sedatives

#### Challenges
- Hundreds of new substances identified annually
- Unknown health effects
- Difficult to detect in standard drug tests
- Legal status varies

### 7. Polysubstance Use

#### Common Combinations
- Fentanyl + xylazine
- Fentanyl + stimulants (speedballing)
- Opioids + benzodiazepines
- Multiple stimulants

#### Risks
- Synergistic effects (more dangerous together)
- Unpredictable interactions
- Harder to treat overdose
- Increased mortality

### 8. Counterfeit Pills

#### What Are They?
- Pills made to look like prescription medications
- Often contain fentanyl
- Sold on illegal markets
- Social media sales increasing

#### Common Fakes
- Oxycodone (M30s)
- Xanax
- Adderall
- Percocet

#### Dangers
- Users think they're getting real medication
- Highly variable fentanyl content
- No quality control
- Major cause of overdose deaths among young people

### 9. New Delivery Methods

#### Social Media Drug Markets
- Snapchat, Instagram, Facebook
- Encrypted messaging apps
- Home delivery
- Anonymous transactions

#### Cryptocurrency Payments
- Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies
- Harder to trace
- Facilitates online sales

#### Vaping Devices
- For cannabis, nicotine, and other drugs
- Discreet use
- Easy to conceal
- Unknown health effects of vaping various substances

### 10. Changing Patterns of Use

#### Telehealth and COVID-19 Impact
- Increased telehealth for treatment
- Changes in drug supply
- Increased isolation
- Economic stressors

#### Rural and Suburban Use
- Drug use spreading beyond urban areas
- Less access to treatment
- Different drug preferences
- Stigma barriers

## Monitoring Systems

### NIDA-Funded Surveillance

#### Monitoring the Future (MTF)
- Annual survey of adolescent drug use
- Tracks trends over time
- Identifies emerging substances

#### National Drug Early Warning System (NDEWS)
- Community-level monitoring
- Sentinel sites across US
- Real-time trend detection

#### Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA)
- Seizure data
- Laboratory testing
- Intelligence on new substances

#### Poison Control Centers
- Exposure reports
- Adverse effects
- Emerging health threats

### International Monitoring
- European Monitoring Centre for Drugs and Drug Addiction (EMCDDA)
- United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC)
- International Narcotics Control Board (INCB)

## Challenges

### Rapid Evolution
- New substances appear faster than they can be regulated
- Chemists modify structures to evade laws
- Difficult to keep up

### Information Gaps
- Unknown health effects
- Limited research
- Delayed recognition of problems

### Treatment Gaps
- No established protocols for new substances
- Limited medication options
- Training needed for providers

### Stigma and Discrimination
- Barriers to seeking help
- Criminalization approaches
- Marginalization of people who use drugs
