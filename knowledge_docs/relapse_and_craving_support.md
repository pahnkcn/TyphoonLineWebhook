## Definitions

### Lapse

A lapse is a brief return to use or a single episode that does not have to become a full return to the old pattern.

### Relapse

A relapse is a more sustained return to the previous pattern of use or a clear loss of the recovery plan.

### Craving

Craving is a strong urge to use. It often rises, peaks, and then falls if the person stays safe and does not act on it immediately.

## Important Principles

- A lapse is not proof of failure.
- Shame increases risk; honest review lowers risk.
- The goal is to interrupt the chain early.
- Safety comes before self-criticism.
- A small plan used quickly is better than a perfect plan used too late.

## Common Trigger Categories

### Emotional Triggers

- loneliness
- anger
- shame
- anxiety
- boredom
- hopelessness

### Situational Triggers

- payday
- being alone late at night
- passing known using locations
- contact from dealers or using friends
- conflict at home
- celebrations or parties

### Physical Triggers

- sleep deprivation
- pain
- withdrawal symptoms
- hunger and dehydration

## Early Warning Signs of Relapse

- thinking one time will be okay
- romanticizing past use
- hiding thoughts from trusted people
- skipping routines that usually help
- increased contact with high-risk people or places
- telling yourself you will deal with it later

## What to Do During a Craving

### 1. Slow the Moment Down

- pause for 5 to 10 minutes
- move away from the trigger if possible
- put distance between yourself and access to the substance

### 2. Regulate the Body

- drink water
- eat something small if hungry
- wash your face or shower
- step outside or change rooms
- do paced breathing

### 3. Ride the Urge

Urge surfing means noticing the craving like a wave:

- name it
- do not fight it aggressively
- watch it rise and fall
- remind yourself it will change with time

### 4. Activate Support

- text or call one safe person
- message a clinic, counselor, sponsor, or peer supporter
- ask someone to stay on the phone or be physically nearby

### 5. Protect the Next Hour

Ask only: what helps me get through the next hour more safely?

## After a Lapse: Immediate Response

- stop the spiral early
- move away from further access if possible
- tell one safe person what happened
- eat, hydrate, and rest if needed
- identify what triggered it
- make one adjustment for the next 24 hours

## Relapse Review Questions

Use these after the immediate risk has passed:

- What happened right before the urge?
- What was I feeling?
- What need was I trying to meet?
- What warning sign did I miss?
- What helped even a little?
- What is one change I can make before the next high-risk moment?

## When to Escalate Beyond Self-Management

Urgent medical or emergency support is needed when relapse involves:

- fentanyl or unknown pills
- overdose concern
- suicidal intent or self-harm risk
- severe withdrawal symptoms
- chest pain, seizure, confusion, or trouble breathing
