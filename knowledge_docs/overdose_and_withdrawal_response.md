## Emergency Rule

If there is **slow breathing, stopped breathing, blue lips, seizure, severe confusion, chest pain, or unresponsiveness**, treat it as an emergency.

## Opioid Overdose: Common Warning Signs

- slow or stopped breathing
- very hard to wake up or unresponsive
- blue or gray lips or fingernails
- pinpoint pupils
- choking, gurgling, or snoring-like sounds
- limp body

## What to Do for Suspected Opioid Overdose

### Step 1

Call emergency services now.

### Step 2

Give naloxone if available.

### Step 3

Try to stimulate the person and support breathing if trained.

### Step 4

Stay with the person. A second naloxone dose may be needed, especially with fentanyl.

### Step 5

If they start breathing again, keep monitoring until professionals take over.

## Important Notes About Naloxone

- naloxone can reverse opioid overdose temporarily
- fentanyl overdose may require multiple doses
- naloxone does not replace emergency medical care
- if xylazine is involved, naloxone may not reverse all sedation, but it should still be given when opioid exposure is suspected

## Fentanyl-Specific Risk

Treat fentanyl exposure as high risk because:

- very small amounts can be lethal
- people may take it unknowingly in counterfeit pills or mixed drugs
- overdose can happen quickly

## Stimulant Emergencies

Urgent care is also needed for stimulant-related danger signs such as:

- chest pain
- extreme agitation or paranoia
- seizure
- severe overheating
- collapse or loss of consciousness

## Withdrawal: When It Needs Medical Attention

### Higher-Risk Situations

Seek urgent medical help when there is:

- confusion or hallucinations
- seizure
- severe dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea
- pregnancy with withdrawal symptoms
- chest pain or trouble breathing
- inability to keep fluids down
- opioid withdrawal with high overdose risk after recent abstinence

## Opioid Withdrawal: Common Symptoms

- muscle aches
- sweating
- chills or goose bumps
- restlessness
- nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea
- insomnia
- anxiety
- strong cravings

Opioid withdrawal is usually extremely uncomfortable and can increase relapse and overdose risk. Professional treatment is safer than trying to manage alone when symptoms are severe.

## Stimulant Crash or Withdrawal

Common symptoms after methamphetamine or stimulant use may include:

- exhaustion
- depression
- irritability
- anxiety
- sleep changes
- increased appetite
- strong cravings

Get urgent help if there is suicidality, psychosis, violent agitation, or inability to stay safe.

## Harm Reduction During High-Risk Use

- do not use alone
- avoid mixing opioids with alcohol or benzodiazepines
- carry naloxone when opioids are involved
- use fentanyl test strips where available
- tell someone to check on you if there is overdose risk

## Safer Next Steps After Immediate Stabilization

- get medical assessment
- contact an addiction treatment service
- consider medication treatment for opioid use disorder
- build a relapse prevention and overdose prevention plan
- replace shame with a concrete safety plan
