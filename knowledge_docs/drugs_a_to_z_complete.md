# Drugs A to Z

Source: [NIDA - Drugs A to Z](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/drugs-a-to-z)

## Overview

Drugs A to Z provides basic information on drugs with addictive potential, including:
- How they are used
- How they make people feel
- Health effects and risks
- Risk for substance use disorder
- Treatment options

---

## Most Searched Drugs

- Cannabis (Marijuana)
- Cocaine
- Fentanyl
- Heroin
- LSD (Acid)
- Meth

---

## A

### Aerosols (Inhalants)
**Also called:** Gluey, Huff, Rush, Whippets

**How used:** Sniffing or snorting fumes, spraying aerosols, huffing from soaked rags, inhaling from balloons

**Effects:** Similar to alcohol or sedatives, short-lasting high, light-headedness, distorted perception

**Health risks:** Nausea, confusion, dizziness, "sudden sniffing death" from heart failure

**Addictive:** Some develop inhalant use disorder

---

### Amphetamines
**Also called:** Bennies, Black Beauties, Speed, Uppers

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II

**Medical use:** Treat ADHD and narcolepsy

**Misuse:** Taking in different doses, without prescription, or with other drugs

**Effects:** Increased energy, euphoria, alertness, talkativeness

**Health risks:** Heart problems, high blood pressure, anxiety, paranoia, psychosis

---

### Anabolic Steroids
**Also called:** Juice, Gym Candy, Pumpers, Stackers

**Medical use:** Treating delayed puberty, muscle loss from diseases

**Misuse:** Taking to build muscle and improve athletic performance

**Effects:** Increased muscle mass, reduced body fat

**Health risks:** Liver damage, heart problems, hormonal changes, aggression ("roid rage")

---

## B

### Barbiturates
**Also called:** Barbs, Reds, Red Birds, Phennies, Tooies, Yellows, Yellow Jackets

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II, III, IV

**Medical use:** Sedatives for anxiety, insomnia, seizures

**Effects:** Relaxation, drowsiness, reduced anxiety

**Health risks:** Overdose, addiction, dangerous withdrawal, respiratory depression

---

### Benzodiazepines
**Also called:** Benzos, Downers

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule IV

**Medical use:** Anxiety, insomnia, seizures, alcohol withdrawal

**Examples:** Valium®, Xanax®, Klonopin®, Ativan®

**Effects:** Relaxation, sedation, reduced anxiety

**Health risks:** Addiction, overdose (especially with opioids), dangerous withdrawal

---

## C

### Cannabis (Marijuana)
**Also called:** BC Bud, Blunt, Dope, Ganja, Grass, Hash, Joint, Kif, Mary Jane, Pot, Reefer, Sinsemilla, Skunk, Smoke, Weed

**Active compounds:** THC (psychoactive), CBD (non-psychoactive)

**How used:** Smoking, vaping, edibles, dabbing

**Effects:** Relaxation, euphoria, altered senses, increased appetite

**Health risks:** Impaired memory and learning, mental health issues, respiratory problems, addiction (9% of users)

**Treatment:** Behavioral therapies

---

### Cocaine
**Also called:** Coca, Coke, Crack, Snow, Soda Cot

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II

**Source:** Leaves of coca plant (Erythroxylon coca)

**How used:** Snorting, smoking (crack), injecting, rubbing on gums

**Effects:** Euphoria, increased energy, alertness, talkativeness

**Health risks:** Heart rhythm disturbances, heart attacks, seizures, stroke, coma, overdose

**Treatment:** No FDA-approved medications; behavioral therapies (CBT, contingency management)

---

### Codeine
**Also called:** Little C, Cody, Captain Cody, Lean, Schoolboy

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II, III

**Medical use:** Mild to moderate pain relief

**How used:** Swallowing tablets or syrup, injecting

**Effects:** Pain relief, euphoria, drowsiness

**Health risks:** Nausea, constipation, respiratory depression, overdose, death

**Treatment:** Methadone, buprenorphine, naltrexone for OUD; behavioral therapies

---

## D

### Delta-8 THC
**Also called:** Delta-8, Delta-8-tetrahydrocannabinol

**Status:** Not FDA-approved, largely unregulated

**How used:** Vaping, edibles, joints, dabbing

**Effects:** Similar to THC - relaxation, increased appetite, anxiety, paranoia

**Health risks:** Medical emergencies including breathing and psychiatric problems, unpredictable effects from contaminants

---

### Depressants
**Also called:** CNS depressants, Central nervous system depressants, Tranquilizers, Sedatives, Anxiolytics, Hypnotics

**Categories:** Barbiturates, benzodiazepines, sleep medications

**Effects:** Slow brain activity, relaxation, drowsiness

**Health risks:** Addiction, overdose, dangerous withdrawal

---

### Dextromethorphan (DXM)
**Also called:** CCC, Dex, DXM, Poor Man's PCP, Robo, Rojo, Skittles, Triple C

**Source:** Cough suppressant in OTC cold medicines

**How used:** Swallowing excessive amounts of cough syrup or tablets

**Effects:** Euphoria, dissociation, hallucinations (at high doses)

**Health risks:** Confusion, dizziness, nausea, increased heart rate, brain damage (at very high doses)

---

### DMT
**Also called:** Businessman's Trip

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule I

**Type:** Powerful psychedelic drug

**Effects:** Intense hallucinations, altered reality, spiritual experiences

**Duration:** Effects last 30-45 minutes when smoked

**Health risks:** Increased heart rate, blood pressure, psychological distress

---

## F

### Fentanyl
**Also called:** Apache, China Girl, China White, Dance Fever, Friend, Goodfellas, Jackpot, Murder 8, Tango & Cash

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II (medical), illicitly manufactured versions are illegal

**Potency:** 50-100 times more potent than morphine

**How used:** Medically as patch or injection; illicitly as powder, in pills, or mixed with other drugs

**Effects:** Intense euphoria, pain relief, drowsiness

**Health risks:** Extremely high overdose risk, respiratory depression, death (2mg can be lethal)

**Treatment:** Naloxone for overdose; methadone, buprenorphine, naltrexone for OUD

---

### Flunitrazepam (Rohypnol®)
**Also called:** Forget Me Pill, Roche, R2, Roofies, Ruffles

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule IV

**Medical use:** Sedative (not approved in US)

**Misuse:** Used as "date rape" drug due to strong sedation effects

**Effects:** Sedation, muscle relaxation, memory impairment

**Health risks:** Overdose, dependence, amnesia

---

## G

### Gases (Inhalants)
**Also called:** Whippets, Chargers

**Examples:** Nitrous oxide, butane, propane

**How used:** Inhaling from balloons, containers, or bags

**Effects:** Short-lasting high, euphoria, distorted perception

**Health risks:** Nausea, confusion, "sudden sniffing death," organ damage

---

### GHB
**Also called:** Sodium Oxybate, Easy Lay, G, Georgia Home Boy, Grievous Bodily Harm, Liquid Ecstasy

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule I, II

**Medical use:** Treatment for narcolepsy (Xyrem®)

**Misuse:** Used as "date rape" drug and party drug

**Effects:** Euphoria, drowsiness, relaxation

**Health risks:** Unconsciousness, seizures, slowed breathing, coma, death

---

## H

### Heroin
**Also called:** Big H, Black Tar, Chiva, Hell Dust, Smack, Thunder

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule I

**Source:** Made from morphine (opium poppy plant)

**Forms:** White/brown powder or black tar

**How used:** Injecting, smoking, snorting

**Effects:** Euphoria, pain relief, drowsiness, "rush"

**Health risks:** Overdose, addiction, infectious diseases (HIV, hepatitis), collapsed veins, brain damage

**Treatment:** Methadone, buprenorphine, naltrexone for OUD; behavioral therapies

---

### Hydrocodone
**Also called:** 357's, Fluff, Hydro, Tabs, Norco, Vic, Vikes, Watsons

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II

**Medical use:** Pain management (Vicodin®, Lortab®)

**How used:** Swallowing tablets

**Effects:** Pain relief, euphoria, drowsiness

**Health risks:** Addiction, overdose, respiratory depression

**Treatment:** Same as other opioids

---

## I

### Inhalants
**Also called:** Gluey, Huff, Rush, Whippets

**Categories:** Solvents, aerosols, gases, nitrites

**Examples:** Glue, paint thinner, gasoline, nitrous oxide

**How used:** Sniffing, snorting, huffing, bagging

**Effects:** Brief intoxication, euphoria, dizziness

**Health risks:** Brain damage, organ damage, "sudden sniffing death"

---

## K

### Ketamine
**Also called:** K, Special K, Cat Valium, Kit Kat, Super Acid

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule III

**Medical use:** Anesthetic

**Misuse:** Club drug, "party and play" drug

**Effects:** Dissociation, hallucinations, pain relief

**Health risks:** Bladder damage, memory loss, addiction, "K-hole" (profound dissociation)

---

### Kratom
**Also called:** Thang, Kakuam, Thom, Ketum, Biak

**Status:** Not scheduled federally, but some states have banned

**Source:** Tropical tree native to Southeast Asia

**How used:** Swallowing as powder, capsules, or tea

**Effects:** Stimulant effects at low doses, opioid-like effects at high doses

**Health risks:** Addiction, liver damage, seizures, withdrawal symptoms

---

## L

### LSD (Acid)
**Also called:** Acid, Blotter Acid, Dots, Mellow Yellow, Window Pane

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule I

**Type:** Powerful hallucinogen

**How used:** Swallowing tablets or blotter paper, liquid drops

**Effects:** Hallucinations, altered perception, spiritual experiences, synesthesia

**Duration:** Effects last 8-12 hours

**Health risks:** Psychological distress, accidents, HPPD (persistent hallucinations)

---

## M

### MDMA (Ecstasy/Molly)
**Also called:** Ecstasy, Molly, Adam, Beans, Clarity, Eve, Hug Drug, X, XTC

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule I

**Type:** Synthetic stimulant with psychedelic properties

**How used:** Swallowing tablets or capsules, snorting

**Effects:** Increased energy, euphoria, emotional warmth, enhanced sensory perception

**Health risks:** Hyperthermia, dehydration, heart problems, anxiety, depression, memory problems

**Treatment:** Behavioral therapies

---

### Methamphetamine
**Also called:** Meth, Batu, Bikers Coffee, Chicken Feed, Crank, Crystal, Glass, Ice, Shabu, Shards, Tina, Yaba

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II

**Forms:** Crystal (ice), powder, pills

**How used:** Smoking, snorting, injecting, swallowing

**Effects:** Intense euphoria, increased energy, alertness, decreased appetite

**Health risks:** Severe dental problems ("meth mouth"), skin sores, heart problems, psychosis, addiction

**Treatment:** Behavioral therapies (Matrix Model, contingency management); no FDA-approved medications

---

### Morphine
**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II

**Medical use:** Severe pain management

**Source:** Natural opiate from opium poppy

**Effects:** Pain relief, euphoria, drowsiness

**Health risks:** Addiction, overdose, respiratory depression

---

## N

### Nitrites (Poppers)
**Also called:** Snappers, Poppers, Rush, Locker Room

**Type:** Inhalants that dilate blood vessels

**Effects:** Warm sensation, dizziness, increased heart rate

**Health risks:** Headaches, unsafe blood pressure drops, methemoglobinemia

---

## O

### Opioids
**Also called:** Narcotics

**Categories:** Natural, synthetic, and semi-synthetic

**Medical use:** Pain relief

**Examples:** Heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone, hydrocodone, morphine, codeine

**Effects:** Pain relief, euphoria, drowsiness

**Health risks:** Addiction, overdose, respiratory depression, death

**Treatment:** Methadone, buprenorphine, naltrexone; naloxone for overdose

---

### Oxycodone
**Also called:** 30s, 40s, Cotton, Greens, Hillbilly Heroin, Killers, OC, Oxy, Roxy

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II

**Medical use:** Pain management (OxyContin®, Percocet®)

**How used:** Swallowing, snorting, injecting

**Effects:** Pain relief, euphoria, sedation

**Health risks:** Highly addictive, overdose, respiratory depression

---

## P

### PCP (Phencyclidine)
**Also called:** Angel Dust, Phencyclidine

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule II

**Type:** Dissociative drug

**How used:** Smoking, swallowing, snorting, injecting

**Effects:** Dissociation, hallucinations, paranoia, aggression

**Health risks:** Psychosis, violent behavior, seizures, coma, death, suicide risk

---

### Psilocybin (Magic Mushrooms)
**Also called:** Magic Mushrooms, Mushrooms, Shrooms

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule I

**Source:** Certain types of mushrooms

**How used:** Eating dried mushrooms, drinking as tea

**Effects:** Hallucinations, altered perception, spiritual experiences

**Health risks:** Psychological distress, misidentification of toxic mushrooms

---

## S

### Synthetic Cannabinoids
**Also called:** Spice, K2, RedX Dawn, Paradise, Demon, Black Magic, Spike, Mr. Nice Guy, Ninja, Zohai, Dream, Genie, Sence, Smoke, Skunk, Serenity, Yucatan, Fire, Skooby Snax, Crazy Clown

**Type:** Lab-created chemicals that mimic THC

**How used:** Smoking or vaping sprayed plant material

**Effects:** Similar to cannabis but unpredictable and often stronger

**Health risks:** Severe agitation, anxiety, paranoia, psychosis, seizures, kidney damage, death

---

### Synthetic Cathinones (Bath Salts)
**Also called:** Bath Salts, Bliss, Blue Silk, Cloud Nine, Ivory Wave, Ocean Burst, Pure Ivory, Purple Wave, Red Dove, Snow Leopard, Vanilla Sky, White Knight, White Lightning

**Type:** Lab-created stimulants similar to cathinone

**How used:** Snorting, swallowing, smoking, injecting

**Effects:** Euphoria, increased energy, alertness, sociability

**Health risks:** Severe agitation, paranoia, hallucinations, violent behavior, heart attack, death

---

## T

### Tobacco/Nicotine
**Also called:** Cigs, Smokes, Chew, Snuff, Dip

**Active ingredient:** Nicotine (highly addictive)

**How used:** Smoking, vaping, chewing, dipping

**Effects:** Stimulation, relaxation, increased focus

**Health risks:** Cancer, heart disease, lung disease, stroke, addiction

**Treatment:** Nicotine replacement therapy (patches, gum, lozenges), bupropion (Zyban®), varenicline (Chantix®)

---

### Tramadol
**DEA Schedule:** Schedule IV

**Medical use:** Mild to moderate pain

**How used:** Swallowing tablets or syrup

**Effects:** Pain relief, euphoria

**Health risks:** Addiction, overdose, respiratory depression, seizures (at high doses)

---

## U

### U-47700 (Pink)
**Also called:** Pinky, Pink, U4

**DEA Schedule:** Schedule I

**Type:** Synthetic opioid

**Potency:** 7.5 times stronger than morphine

**How used:** Swallowing, snorting, injecting

**Effects:** Pain relief, euphoria

**Health risks:** High overdose risk, respiratory depression, death

---

## X

### Xylazine
**Also called:** Tranq, Tranq Dope, Zombie Drug

**Status:** Veterinary sedative (not approved for human use)

**Type:** Non-opioid sedative

**How used:** Often mixed with fentanyl without users' knowledge

**Effects:** Heavy sedation, decreased breathing, low blood pressure

**Health risks:** Severe skin wounds and infections, overdose (naloxone does NOT reverse xylazine)

## Treatment Options Summary

### For Opioid Use Disorder
- **Methadone** - Long-acting opioid agonist
- **Buprenorphine** - Partial agonist
- **Naltrexone** - Opioid antagonist
- **Behavioral therapies** - CBT, contingency management

### For Stimulant Use Disorder
- **No FDA-approved medications**
- **Behavioral therapies** - Matrix Model, CBT, contingency management

### For Sedative Use Disorder
- **Gradual tapering** under medical supervision
- **Behavioral therapies**

### For Cannabis Use Disorder
- **Behavioral therapies**
- **No FDA-approved medications**

### For Hallucinogen Use Disorder
- **Behavioral therapies**
- **Supportive care**

## Emergency Information

### Overdose Signs
- Slow or stopped breathing
- Blue lips or fingernails
- Unresponsiveness
- Pinpoint pupils
- Cold, clammy skin

### What to Do
1. Call 911 immediately
2. Administer naloxone if available (for opioid overdose)
3. Stay with the person
4. Perform rescue breathing if trained
5. Place in recovery position
