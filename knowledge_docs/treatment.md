# Treatment

Source: [NIDA - Treatment](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/treatment)

## Overview

Treatment for substance use disorders is effective and helps people recover to lead healthy, productive lives. NIDA supports research to develop and improve treatments for drug addiction.

## Key Principles of Effective Treatment

### 1. Addiction is a Treatable Disease
- Addiction is a complex but treatable disease
- Affects brain function and behavior
- No single treatment is appropriate for everyone

### 2. Treatment Must be Readily Available
- Access to treatment when individuals are ready
- Reducing barriers to entry
- Immediate intervention when possible

### 3. Treatment Addresses Multiple Needs
- Not just drug use
- Medical, psychological, social, vocational, and legal problems
- Tailored to individual needs

### 4. Treatment Duration Matters
- Minimum of 3 months for significant improvement
- Longer durations associated with better outcomes
- Chronic disease requiring ongoing management

### 5. Behavioral Therapies are Essential
- Most commonly used form of treatment
- Can include individual, family, or group counseling
- Address motivation, coping skills, and relapse prevention

### 6. Medications are Important
- Especially for opioid, alcohol, and nicotine addiction
- Combined with behavioral therapies for best outcomes
- FDA-approved medications available

### 7. Treatment Plans Must be Assessed and Modified
- Continuous monitoring of progress
- Adjustments as needs change
- Flexibility in approach

## Types of Treatment

### Detoxification (Detox)
- Medically supervised withdrawal
- Manages acute physical symptoms
- First step, not treatment by itself
- Prepares for ongoing treatment

### Residential Treatment
- 24-hour structured care
- Length varies (30 days to 1 year)
- Intensive therapy and support
- Removes from triggers and environment

### Outpatient Treatment
- Live at home while attending treatment
- Various intensity levels
- Individual and group counseling
- Flexible scheduling

### Intensive Outpatient Programs (IOP)
- More frequent sessions than standard outpatient
- Several hours per day, multiple days per week
- Structured while maintaining daily responsibilities

### Partial Hospitalization Programs (PHP)
- Day treatment programs
- Medical monitoring
- Intensive therapy
- Return home evenings

## Behavioral Therapies

### Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
- Helps patients recognize and avoid triggers
- Develops coping strategies
- Changes harmful thought patterns
- Effective for multiple substances

### Contingency Management
- Provides tangible rewards for positive behaviors
- Voucher-based reinforcement
- Prize-based incentives
- Proven effective for stimulant use disorders

### Motivational Enhancement Therapy (MET)
- Builds motivation to change
- Rapid, internally motivated change
- Non-confrontational approach

### 12-Step Programs
- Based on Alcoholics Anonymous model
- Peer support and mutual help
- Spiritual component
- Narcotics Anonymous, Cocaine Anonymous

### Family Therapy
- Addresses family dynamics
- Improves communication
- Builds support system
- Includes Multidimensional Family Therapy (MDFT)

### The Matrix Model
- Comprehensive 16-week program
- For stimulant use disorders
- Combines multiple therapeutic approaches
- Individual counseling, group therapy, family education

## Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT)

### For Opioid Use Disorder

#### Methadone
- **Type:** Long-acting opioid agonist
- **How it works:** Reduces cravings and withdrawal symptoms
- **Administration:** Daily at specialized clinics
- **Benefits:** Stabilizes patients, reduces illicit opioid use
- **Considerations:** Requires daily clinic visits initially

#### Buprenorphine
- **Type:** Partial opioid agonist
- **Brand names:** Suboxone®, Subutex®, Sublocade®
- **How it works:** Reduces cravings, blocks effects of other opioids
- **Administration:** Sublingual tablets, films, or monthly injection
- **Benefits:** Office-based prescribing, lower overdose risk
- **Considerations:** Requires special training to prescribe

#### Naltrexone
- **Type:** Opioid antagonist
- **Brand names:** Vivitrol®, Revia®
- **How it works:** Blocks opioid effects
- **Administration:** Oral (daily) or extended-release injectable (monthly)
- **Benefits:** No opioid effects, non-addictive
- **Considerations:** Requires detoxification before starting

### For Alcohol Use Disorder

#### Naltrexone
- Blocks alcohol euphoria
- Reduces cravings
- Oral or injectable forms

#### Acamprosate (Campral®)
- Reduces protracted withdrawal symptoms
- Helps maintain abstinence
- For alcohol-dependent patients

#### Disulfiram (Antabuse®)
- Causes unpleasant reaction when alcohol consumed
- Deterrent to drinking
- Requires motivation and supervision

### For Nicotine Addiction

#### Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT)
- Patches, gum, lozenges, nasal spray, inhaler
- Reduces withdrawal symptoms
- Available over-the-counter

#### Bupropion (Zyban®)
- Antidepressant that reduces cravings
- Non-nicotine medication
- Prescription required

#### Varenicline (Chantix®)
- Reduces cravings and withdrawal
- Blocks nicotine effects
- Prescription required

### For Stimulant Use Disorders
- **Currently no FDA-approved medications**
- Research ongoing
- Behavioral therapies are first-line treatment

## Treatment Settings

### Hospital-Based Programs
- Medical stabilization
- Co-occurring medical conditions
- Intensive monitoring

### Residential Facilities
- Therapeutic communities
- Long-term residential treatment
- Recovery housing

### Outpatient Clinics
- Regular counseling sessions
- Medication management
- Group therapy

### Telehealth
- Remote counseling
- Medication management
- Increased access
- Expanded during COVID-19

## Specialized Treatment Programs

### For Adolescents
- Age-appropriate approaches
- Family involvement
- Educational support
- Peer influence management

### For Pregnant Women
- Medication-assisted treatment (methadone or buprenorphine)
- Prenatal care coordination
- Postpartum support
- Neonatal abstinence syndrome management

### For Criminal Justice Populations
- Treatment while incarcerated
- Reentry planning
- Continuing care in community
- Drug courts

### For Co-occurring Disorders
- Integrated treatment for substance use and mental health
- Addressing both simultaneously
- Specialized staff training

## Recovery Support Services

### Recovery Housing
- Drug-free living environments
- Peer support
- Structure and accountability
- Bridge to independent living

### Recovery Coaches
- Peer support specialists
- Personal experience with recovery
- Guidance and motivation
- Connection to resources

### Mutual Help Groups
- Alcoholics Anonymous (AA)
- Narcotics Anonymous (NA)
- SMART Recovery
- Secular Organizations for Sobriety

### Employment Support
- Vocational training
- Job placement
- Workplace support
- Career counseling

## Measuring Treatment Success

### Outcomes
- Reduced substance use
- Improved health
- Better functioning
- Reduced criminal activity
- Improved relationships
- Employment and education

### Relapse
- Common part of recovery
- Not treatment failure
- Signal to adjust treatment
- Learning opportunity

## Barriers to Treatment

### Individual Barriers
- Stigma and shame
- Denial of problem
- Cost concerns
- Lack of insurance
- Fear of withdrawal

### System Barriers
- Limited availability
- Long waitlists
- Geographic distance
- Lack of specialized programs
- Insurance limitations

### Addressing Barriers
- Reducing stigma through education
- Expanding insurance coverage
- Increasing treatment capacity
- Telehealth expansion
- Harm reduction approaches
