# Methamphetamine

Source: [NIDA - Methamphetamine](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/methamphetamine)

## What is Methamphetamine?

Methamphetamine is a **powerful, highly addictive stimulant** that affects the central nervous system. It is chemically similar to amphetamine, a drug used to treat ADHD and narcolepsy.

### Common Names
- Meth
- Crystal
- Ice
- Glass
- Crank
- Speed
- Chalk
- Tina
- Yaba
- Shabu
- Batu
- Bikers Coffee
- Chicken Feed
- Shards

## Forms of Methamphetamine

### 1. Crystal Methamphetamine
- Clear, crystal-like chunks
- Most potent form
- Smoked or injected
- Also called "ice" or "glass"

### 2. Powder Methamphetamine
- White, bitter powder
- Can be snorted, swallowed, or injected
- Also called "speed" or "crank"

### 3. Pills/Tablets
- Yaba (popular in Southeast Asia)
- Often colored and flavored
- Swallowed or smoked

## How Do People Use Methamphetamine?

- **Smoking** - Produces immediate, intense effects
- **Swallowing** - Delayed onset, longer duration
- **Snorting** - Effects within 3-5 minutes
- **Injecting** - Immediate, intense rush

## Short-term Health Effects

### Immediate Effects
- Increased wakefulness and physical activity
- Decreased appetite
- Faster breathing
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Increased blood pressure and body temperature
- Euphoria and rush of energy
- Increased focus and alertness

### Negative Short-term Effects
- Anxiety and confusion
- Insomnia
- Mood disturbances
- Violent behavior
- Psychotic features:
  - Paranoia
  - Hallucinations
  - Delusions

## Long-term Health Effects

### Physical Effects
- **Extreme weight loss** - Severe malnutrition
- **Severe dental problems** - "Meth mouth"
  - Tooth decay
  - Gum disease
  - Tooth loss
- **Skin sores** - From scratching and picking
- **Increased risk of infectious diseases** (HIV, hepatitis B and C)
  - From needle sharing
  - From risky sexual behavior
- **Cardiovascular problems**
  - Irregular heartbeat
  - Heart attack
  - Stroke
- **Respiratory problems** - From smoking
- **Organ damage** - Liver, kidney, lung damage

### Mental Health Effects
- **Addiction** - Highly addictive substance
- **Anxiety and depression**
- **Confusion and memory loss**
- **Sleep disturbances**
- **Violent behavior**
- **Psychosis** - Can persist for months or years after stopping use
  - Paranoia
  - Visual and auditory hallucinations
  - Delusions (e.g., feeling of insects crawling under skin)
  - Repetitive motor activity

### Brain Changes
- Changes in brain structure and function
- Deficits in thinking and motor skills
- Reduced dopamine transporters
- Impaired verbal learning
- Reduced coordination

## Methamphetamine and HIV

### Increased Risk
- Methamphetamine use increases risk of HIV infection
- Associated with risky sexual behaviors
- Impairs judgment and decision-making
- May increase HIV replication in the body

### Impact on Treatment
- Methamphetamine users with HIV may have:
  - Reduced effectiveness of antiretroviral therapy
  - Faster disease progression
  - Greater cognitive impairment

## Methamphetamine Use Disorder

### Signs of Addiction
- Unable to stop using despite wanting to
- Spending excessive time obtaining, using, or recovering from meth
- Neglecting responsibilities
- Continuing use despite negative consequences
- Developing tolerance (needing more to get same effect)
- Experiencing withdrawal symptoms when stopping

### Withdrawal Symptoms
- Depression
- Anxiety
- Fatigue
- Increased appetite
- Agitation
- Excessive sleepiness
- Vivid, unpleasant dreams
- Slowed thinking and movement
- Strong cravings

## Treatment Options

### Current Status
**There are currently no FDA-approved medications** specifically for methamphetamine use disorder. However, effective behavioral treatments exist.

### Behavioral Therapies

#### 1. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
- Helps patients recognize and avoid triggers
- Develops coping strategies
- Changes harmful thought patterns

#### 2. Contingency Management
- Provides tangible rewards for staying drug-free
- Uses positive reinforcement
- Has shown effectiveness in clinical trials

#### 3. The Matrix Model
- 16-week comprehensive behavioral treatment
- Combines:
  - Behavioral therapy
  - Family education
  - Individual counseling
  - 12-Step support
  - Drug testing
  - Encouragement for non-drug-related activities

#### 4. Motivational Incentives for Enhancing Drug Abuse Recovery (MIEDAR)
- Incentive-based method
- Promotes abstinence from methamphetamine
- Demonstrated efficacy through clinical trials

### Medications in Development

#### 1. AV411 (Ibudilast)
- Suppresses neuroinflammatory actions of glial cells
- Inhibits methamphetamine self-administration in rats
- Being fast-tracked in clinical trials

#### 2. Monoclonal Antibodies
- Anti-methamphetamine antibody (mAb7F9)
- Neutralizes drug in bloodstream before reaching brain
- Clinical studies underway to establish safety

#### 3. Vaccines
- Stimulate immune system to produce antibodies
- Block methamphetamine effects
- In development

## Methamphetamine Use in Pregnancy

### Risks to Mother and Baby
- Premature birth
- Low birth weight
- Developmental problems
- Withdrawal symptoms in newborns
- Increased risk of placental abruption

### Long-term Effects on Children
- Behavioral problems
- Learning difficulties
- Attention deficits
- Emotional regulation issues

## Statistics and Trends

### Usage Data
- Methamphetamine use has increased in recent years
- Significant problem in rural and urban areas
- Increasing purity and decreasing prices
- Growing problem in many regions

### Overdose Deaths
- Overdose deaths involving methamphetamine have increased
- Often involves combination with other drugs, especially fentanyl

## Harm Reduction

### Strategies
- Never use alone
- Use clean needles if injecting
- Get tested for HIV and hepatitis
- Practice safe sex
- Seek treatment for dental problems
- Stay hydrated and nourished

## Recovery

### Is Recovery Possible?
**Yes!** While methamphetamine is highly addictive, recovery is possible with:
- Appropriate treatment
- Strong support system
- Long-term commitment
- Relapse prevention strategies

### Brain Recovery
- Some brain changes may improve after stopping use
- Recovery can take months or years
- Cognitive function may improve with sustained abstinence
