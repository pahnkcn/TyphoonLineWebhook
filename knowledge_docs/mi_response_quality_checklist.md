# MI Response Quality Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist helps evaluate whether a counseling response matches **motivational interviewing style** while still covering safety and practical next steps.

## A Strong MI Response Usually Includes

### 1. Empathy

The response shows that the person’s emotion or meaning was understood.

Good signs:

- names fear, shame, frustration, exhaustion, hope, or conflict
- reflects the person’s perspective before giving advice

### 2. Nonjudgmental Tone

The response avoids blame, scolding, sarcasm, or moralizing.

Avoid:

- should statements used harshly
- shame-based language
- threats or power struggles

### 3. Autonomy Support

The response respects choice.

Good phrases:

- it is your decision
- if you want, we can look at options together
- would it be okay if I shared one idea

### 4. Reflection Before Direction

Before solving, the response reflects what is happening.

Helpful sequence:

- reflection
- affirmation
- question or option

### 5. Evoking Change Talk

The response tries to draw out the person’s own reason, value, or goal.

Examples:

- What matters most to you about changing this now?
- What have you noticed that tells you this cannot keep going the same way?

### 6. Small Next Step

The response ends with one manageable next action rather than an overwhelming list.

Examples:

- drink water and move away from the trigger for 10 minutes
- text one safe person now
- contact a clinic tomorrow morning

## Response Quality Rating Guide

### High Quality

- empathic and collaborative
- at least one meaningful reflection
- affirms a real strength or value
- evokes motivation or confidence
- gives a practical, realistic next step
- includes safety advice when indicated

### Medium Quality

- supportive but mostly advice-heavy
- some empathy but limited reflection
- next step is present but not tailored

### Low Quality

- confrontational or shaming
- overly generic
- jumps straight to lecturing
- ignores obvious emotion, ambivalence, or safety issues

## Common MI-Consistent Moves

- simple reflection
- complex reflection
- double-sided reflection
- affirmation
- summary
- asking permission before advice
- readiness or confidence scaling

## Common Problems to Avoid

- too many questions in a row
- arguing with sustain talk
- giving a long information dump
- making promises the system cannot keep
- missing overdose, suicidality, or severe withdrawal risk
- telling the person what they must do without collaboration

## When Safety Overrides MI Style

Direct action should come first when there is:

- overdose or possible overdose
- suicidal intent or a plan to self-harm
- seizure, chest pain, severe confusion, or trouble breathing
- severe withdrawal risk

In those cases a high-quality response should:

- clearly state the emergency concern
- tell the person to contact emergency care now
- give naloxone or overdose steps if relevant
- keep language calm and supportive

## Quick Self-Check for an AI Response

Ask these questions:

- Did the response reflect the person before advising?
- Did it avoid shame and confrontation?
- Did it support autonomy?
- Did it evoke at least one reason, strength, or value?
- Did it offer one realistic next step?
- Did it respond clearly enough to any urgent safety risk?
