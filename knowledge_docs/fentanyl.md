# Fentanyl

Source: [NIDA - Fentanyl](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/fentanyl)

## What is Fentanyl?

Fentanyl is a **powerful synthetic opioid** that is:
- **50-100 times more potent than morphine**
- **50 times more potent than heroin**
- Used medically for severe pain management
- Also manufactured and distributed illegally

## Medical Use vs. Illicit Use

### FDA-Approved Fentanyl Products
- Used during surgery
- Treating post-surgical pain
- Managing pain in opioid-tolerant patients
- Available as patches, lozenges, and injectable forms

### Illicitly Manufactured Fentanyl (IMF)
- Made in clandestine labs
- Often mixed with other drugs without users' knowledge
- Major driver of overdose deaths in the United States
- Can be sold as powder, pressed into pills, or mixed with other substances

## The Overdose Crisis

### Alarming Statistics
- Drug overdose deaths exceeded **100,000** in a 12-month period (April 2020 - April 2021)
- **28.5% increase** over the previous year
- **64% of overdose deaths** involved synthetic opioids (mainly illicit fentanyl)
- Synthetic opioid deaths increased by **55% in 2020**

### Regional Impact (2020 Data)
- **Northeast:** 3/5% increase; 5,194 deaths
- **Midwest:** 33.1% increase; 2,010 deaths
- **South:** 64.7% increase; 4,342 deaths
- **West:** 93.9% increase; 1,852 deaths

## How Fentanyl Causes Overdose

### Potency
- Extremely small amounts can be fatal
- 2 milligrams (about the size of a few grains of salt) can be lethal
- Often mixed with other drugs, unbeknownst to users

### Polydrug Use
Fentanyl is frequently found in:
- Heroin
- Cocaine
- Methamphetamine
- Counterfeit prescription pills (fake OxyContin, Xanax, etc.)

### Unique Challenges
- **Rapid onset:** Effects occur very quickly
- **Chest wall rigidity:** Can make breathing difficult
- **Renarcotization:** Overdose can recur after initial naloxone reversal

## Health Risks

### Short-term Effects
- Extreme euphoria
- Drowsiness
- Nausea
- Confusion
- Constipation
- Sedation
- Respiratory depression
- Unconsciousness
- Coma
- Death

### Long-term Effects
- Tolerance
- Physical dependence
- Addiction
- Brain changes affecting decision-making and behavior

## Recognizing Fentanyl Overdose

### Signs and Symptoms
- Pinpoint pupils
- Slow or stopped breathing
- Blue lips and fingernails
- Cold, clammy skin
- Unresponsiveness
- Gurgling sounds or choking
- Limp body

## Overdose Response

### Naloxone (Narcan®)
- Can reverse fentanyl overdose
- May require **multiple doses** due to fentanyl's potency
- Should be administered immediately
- Call 911 even if naloxone is given

### Good Samaritan Laws
Many states have laws protecting people who call 911 during an overdose from prosecution for minor drug-related offenses.

## NIDA Research on Fentanyl

### Current Research Areas

#### 1. Fentanyl Test Strips
- Developing accurate and easy-to-use test strips
- Can detect illicit fentanyl in drug samples
- Allows users to make informed decisions

#### 2. Monoclonal Antibodies
- Research to develop antibodies that prevent fentanyl from reaching the brain
- Could block respiratory depression and overdose
- Targeting various fentanyl analogs (carfentanil, alfentanil, sufentanil, acetylfentanil)

#### 3. Small Molecule Sequestrants
- Rapid overdose reversal agents
- Bind to fentanyl molecules in blood
- Accelerate removal through urine

#### 4. Vaccines
- Stimulate immune system to produce antibodies
- Neutralize fentanyl before it reaches the brain
- Currently in development

### HEAL Initiative Research
NIDA leads research through the NIH HEAL Initiative focusing on:
- Understanding fentanyl's effects on the body
- Developing treatments for fentanyl dependence and addiction
- Improving overdose reversal methods
- Addressing unique challenges of fentanyl overdoses

## Harm Reduction Strategies

### 1. Fentanyl Test Strips
- Detect presence of fentanyl in drugs
- Available through harm reduction programs

### 2. Naloxone Distribution
- Making naloxone widely available
- Training family and friends to use it
- Carrying naloxone if using drugs

### 3. Never Use Alone
- Using with others who can respond to overdose
- Hotlines and apps that provide remote monitoring

### 4. Safe Consumption Spaces
- Supervised settings where overdose can be immediately treated
- Available in some countries and cities

## Treatment for Fentanyl Use Disorder

### Medications
- **Methadone** - Long-acting opioid agonist
- **Buprenorphine** - Partial agonist, blocks other opioids
- **Naltrexone** - Blocks opioid effects

### Challenges
- Higher buprenorphine doses may be needed for treatment induction
- More severe and prolonged withdrawal symptoms
- Requires specialized treatment approaches

### Behavioral Therapies
- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
- Contingency Management
- Motivational Interviewing
- Group counseling

## Prevention and Education

### Public Health Approaches
- Education about risks of counterfeit pills
- Warning about drugs mixed with fentanyl
- Promoting naloxone access
- Reducing stigma to encourage treatment seeking

### Law Enforcement Response
- Seizing illicit fentanyl
- Tracking distribution networks
- Collaborating with public health

## Key Facts to Remember

1. **No safe way to use illicit fentanyl**
2. **Any drug purchased illegally may contain fentanyl**
3. **Counterfeit pills are increasingly common**
4. **Naloxone can save lives - carry it and know how to use it**
5. **Treatment works - recovery is possible**
