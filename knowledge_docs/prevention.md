# Prevention

Source: [NIDA - Prevention](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/prevention)

## Overview

Prevention is a critical component of addressing drug use and addiction. NIDA supports research to identify effective strategies for preventing substance use initiation and progression to addiction.

## Key Prevention Principles

### Risk and Protective Factors

#### Risk Factors (Increase Likelihood of Drug Use)
- **Individual factors:**
  - Early aggressive behavior
  - Lack of self-control
  - Difficult temperament
  - History of abuse or neglect
  - Poor social skills
  - Academic failure

- **Family factors:**
  - Parental substance use
  - Family conflict
  - Lack of parental supervision
  - Parental attitudes favorable to drug use

- **Peer factors:**
  - Association with drug-using peers
  - Peer rejection

- **School factors:**
  - Poor classroom management
  - Academic failure
  - Bullying

- **Community factors:**
  - Drug availability
  - Community poverty
  - Neighborhood disorganization
  - Low community attachment

#### Protective Factors (Decrease Likelihood of Drug Use)
- **Individual factors:**
  - Positive self-image
  - Self-control
  - Social competence
  - Academic success

- **Family factors:**
  - Parental monitoring and support
  - Clear family rules
  - Positive family relationships

- **Peer factors:**
  - Association with positive peers
  - Involvement in positive activities

- **School factors:**
  - School connectedness
  - Positive school climate
  - Academic success

- **Community factors:**
  - Community norms against drug use
  - Availability of recreational activities
  - Strong community institutions

## Prevention Approaches

### Universal Prevention
Targets entire populations (e.g., all students in a school)
- School-based curricula
- Community-wide media campaigns
- Environmental strategies

### Selective Prevention
Targets subgroups at higher risk
- Children of parents with substance use disorders
- Youth in high-risk neighborhoods
- Students with academic problems

### Indicated Prevention
Targets individuals already showing early signs
- Youth experimenting with substances
- Individuals with conduct problems
- Those showing mental health symptoms

## Evidence-Based Prevention Programs

### School-Based Programs

#### Life Skills Training (LST)
- Teaches personal self-management skills
- Social skills
- Drug resistance skills
- Proven effective in reducing tobacco, alcohol, and drug use

#### Project ALERT
- Middle school-based program
- Focuses on alcohol, tobacco, and marijuana
- Teaches resistance skills and consequences

#### Strengthening Families Program
- For families with children ages 6-16
- Improves parenting skills
- Enhances family communication
- Reduces substance use

### Family-Based Programs

#### Functional Family Therapy (FFT)
- For at-risk youth
- Improves family relationships
- Addresses behavioral problems

#### Multidimensional Family Therapy (MDFT)
- Comprehensive family-based treatment
- Addresses multiple domains
- Effective for adolescent substance use

### Community-Based Programs

#### Communities That Care (CTC)
- Community prevention system
- Uses data to guide selection of evidence-based programs
- Reduces youth substance use and delinquency

#### PROSPER (PROmoting School-community-university Partnerships to Enhance Resilience)
- Partnership model
- Delivers evidence-based programs
- Sustains prevention efforts

## Prevention Strategies

### Education and Information
- Providing accurate information about drugs and their effects
- Correcting misconceptions
- Teaching decision-making skills

### Skills Development
- Refusal skills
- Communication skills
- Problem-solving skills
- Stress management
- Goal setting

### Environmental Strategies
- Restricting access to drugs
- Enforcing laws and policies
- Changing social norms
- Increasing prices (through taxation)

### Policy Approaches
- Minimum legal drinking ages
- Prescription drug monitoring programs
- Smoke-free policies
- Drug-free school zones

- Universal screening for substance use

## Effectiveness of Prevention

### Research Findings
- Every dollar invested in prevention saves money in treatment and healthcare costs
- Effective prevention delays initiation and reduces progression
- Long-term benefits extend into adulthood
- Prevention improves multiple outcomes (academic, social, health)

### Cost-Effectiveness
- Prevention programs show positive return on investment
- Savings in healthcare, criminal justice, and productivity
- Benefits outweigh costs
