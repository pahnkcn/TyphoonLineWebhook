# Opioids

Source: [NIDA - Opioids](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/opioids)

## What are Opioids?

Opioids are a class of drugs that include:
- **Prescription pain relievers** - oxycodone (OxyContin®), hydrocodone (Vicodin®), codeine, morphine
- **Synthetic opioids** - fentanyl, methadone, tramadol
- **Illegal drugs** - heroin

## How Do Opioids Work?

Opioids work by binding to and activating opioid receptors on cells in the brain, spinal cord, and other organs. When opioids attach to these receptors, they:
- Block pain signals sent from the body through the spinal cord to the brain
- Release large amounts of dopamine throughout the body
- Produce feelings of pleasure and pain relief

## How Do Opioids Make People Feel?

### Short-term Effects
- Pain relief
- Euphoria (intense happiness or feelings of well-being)
- Drowsiness
- Confusion
- Nausea
- Constipation
- Slowed breathing

### Effects at Higher Doses
- Increased sensitivity to pain (after long-term use)
- Respiratory depression (breathing too slowly or shallowly)
- Overdose and death

## Health Risks of Using Opioids

### Physical Risks
- **Respiratory Depression:** Slowed breathing that can lead to suffocation
- **Overdose:** Taking too much can slow breathing to dangerous levels
- **Constipation:** Common side effect that can be severe
- **Nausea and vomiting**
- **Increased sensitivity to pain** after long-term use
- **Hormonal disruption**
- **Weakened immune system**

### Mental Health Risks
- Depression
- Anxiety
- Confusion
- Impaired judgment

### Other Risks
- **Addiction:** Opioids are highly addictive
- **Dependence:** Body becomes reliant on the drug to function
- **Tolerance:** Needing more of the drug to achieve the same effect
- **Withdrawal symptoms** when stopping use

## What is an Overdose?

An opioid overdose occurs when a person takes too much of an opioid, causing:
- Slowed or stopped breathing
- Blue lips or fingernails
- Unresponsiveness
- Pinpoint pupils
- Cold, clammy skin

### Overdose Reversal
**Naloxone (Narcan®)** is a medication that can rapidly reverse opioid overdose by:
- Binding to opioid receptors
- Blocking the effects of opioids
- Restoring normal breathing

Naloxone is available as:
- Nasal spray
- Injectable solution
- Auto-injector

## Opioid Use Disorder (OUD)

### Signs and Symptoms
- Taking opioids in larger amounts or for longer than prescribed
- Unsuccessful attempts to cut down or control use
- Spending significant time obtaining, using, or recovering from opioids
- Craving opioids
- Failure to fulfill obligations at work, school, or home
- Continued use despite social or interpersonal problems
- Giving up important activities
- Using in physically hazardous situations
- Continued use despite physical or psychological problems
- Tolerance
- Withdrawal symptoms

### Withdrawal Symptoms
- Muscle and bone pain
- Cold flashes with goose bumps
- Cramps
- Diarrhea
- Involuntary leg movements
- Restlessness
- Vomiting
- Insomnia
- Severe cravings

## Treatment Options

### FDA-Approved Medications for OUD

#### 1. Methadone
- Long-acting opioid agonist
- Reduces cravings and withdrawal symptoms
- Dispensed through specialized clinics
- Taken daily

#### 2. Buprenorphine
- Partial opioid agonist
- Available in various formulations (Suboxone®, Subutex®)
- Can be prescribed in office settings
- Blocks effects of other opioids

#### 3. Naltrexone
- Opioid antagonist
- Blocks opioid effects
- Available as oral (daily) or extended-release injectable (monthly)
- Requires detoxification before starting

### Lofexidine
- FDA-approved to reduce opioid withdrawal symptoms
- Non-opioid medication
- Helps manage withdrawal discomfort

### Behavioral Treatments
- **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)**
- **Contingency Management**
- **Motivational Interviewing**
- **Group therapy and counseling**
- **12-step programs**

## The Opioid Crisis

### Statistics
- Drug overdose deaths exceeded 100,000 in a 12-month period (2020-2021)
- Synthetic opioids (primarily illicit fentanyl) are the main driver
- 64% of overdose deaths involved synthetic opioids

### HEAL Initiative
NIDA leads research through the NIH HEAL Initiative to:
- Develop new treatments for pain and addiction
- Improve overdose prevention
- Enhance treatment delivery
- Reduce stigma

## Prevention Strategies

### Safe Prescribing Practices
- Following CDC guidelines for opioid prescribing
- Using prescription drug monitoring programs
- Screening for risk factors

### Harm Reduction
- Naloxone distribution programs
- Fentanyl test strips
- Safe disposal of unused medications
- Syringe services programs

### Education
- Public awareness campaigns
- Healthcare provider training
- Patient education on risks and safe use
