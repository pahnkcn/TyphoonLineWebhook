# Cannabis (Marijuana)

Source: [NIDA - Cannabis (Marijuana)](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/cannabis-marijuana)

## Overview

NIDA funds research on the health effects of cannabis products, including impacts on the developing brain and on mental health. The institute supports research on the therapeutic potential of cannabis and cannabinoids while also studying the risks associated with use.

## What is Cannabis?

Cannabis refers to a group of three plants with psychoactive properties, known as *Cannabis sativa*, *Cannabis indica*, and *Cannabis ruderalis*. When the flowers of these plants are harvested and dried, you're left with one of the most common drugs in the world.

### Common Names
- Marijuana
- Weed
- Pot
- Grass
- Bud
- Herb
- Mary Jane
- Ganja
- Dope

## Key Components

### THC (Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol)
- The main psychoactive compound in cannabis
- Responsible for the "high" feeling
- Can increase appetite and reduce nausea
- May decrease pain, inflammation, and muscle control problems

### CBD (Cannabidiol)
- Non-psychoactive compound
- Does not produce a "high"
- Potential therapeutic benefits for pain, inflammation, and seizures
- FDA approved Epidiolex® (CBD-based) for treatment of severe childhood epilepsy

## Health Effects

### Short-term Effects
- Altered senses (for example, seeing brighter colors)
- Altered sense of time
- Changes in mood
- Impaired body movement
- Difficulty with thinking and problem-solving
- Impaired memory
- Hallucinations (when taken in high doses)
- Delusions (when taken in high doses)
- Psychosis (risk is highest with regular use of high-potency marijuana)

### Long-term Effects
- **Brain Development:** Marijuana use during pregnancy or among adolescents may affect brain development
- **Mental Health:** Links to depression, anxiety, and suicidal thoughts among teens
- **Respiratory Issues:** Smoking marijuana can irritate the lungs
- **Addiction:** Approximately 9% of people who use marijuana will become addicted

### Effects on the Developing Brain
Research from the ABCD Study shows that marijuana use during adolescence may:
- Affect brain development
- Impact learning and memory
- Alter attention and cognitive function
- Increase risk of mental health disorders

## Therapeutic Potential

### FDA-Approved Medications
1. **Dronabinol (Marinol®)** - Treats nausea from chemotherapy and appetite loss in AIDS patients
2. **Nabilone (Cesamet®)** - Treats chemotherapy-induced nausea
3. **Epidiolex®** - CBD-based medication for severe childhood epilepsy (Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome)

### Research Areas
- Pain management
- Multiple sclerosis symptoms
- Epilepsy treatment
- PTSD and anxiety disorders
- Cancer-related symptoms
- Inflammatory conditions

## Cannabis Policy Research

NIDA supports research on the effects of cannabis policy changes, including:
- Impact of legalization on use patterns
- Public health outcomes
- Youth access and use
- Driving under the influence
- Workplace safety

### Standardized Dosing
NIDA has adopted a **standard 5mg unit of THC** for research studies to ensure consistency across studies and facilitate translation of findings into policy and clinical practice.

## Key Research Findings

### From the ABCD Study
- Cannabis is one of the most-used drugs by teenagers
- A quarter of 12th graders report use in the past year
- Reduction in teenage use of substances in general, including cannabis
- Increases in disapproval of cannabis use and perception of its harms

### From the HBCD Study
The Healthy Brain and Child Development (HBCD) study is tracking impacts of prenatal exposure to cannabis and other environmental influences on the developing brain.

## Harm Reduction

### Points to Remember
- Cannabis contains many active constituents and is not the same as THC
- CBD shows therapeutic potential without psychoactive effects
- Pregnant women should not use cannabis without consulting healthcare providers
- Adolescents are particularly vulnerable to cannabis's effects on brain development
- Products should have accurate labeling of contents
