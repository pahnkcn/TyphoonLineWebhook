# Motivational Interviewing Core Skills

## Purpose

This guide is a compact RAG-ready reference for **motivational interviewing (MI)** in substance-use counseling. It is designed for short chat responses, brief interventions, and supportive follow-up conversations.

## Aliases and Retrieval Terms

- Motivational interviewing
- MI
- สัมภาษณ์เสริมแรงจูงใจ
- เทคนิคเสริมแรงจูงใจ
- คุยแบบไม่ตัดสิน
- OARS
- reflective listening
- listening with empathy

## When to Use MI

Use MI when the person:

- feels ambivalent about stopping or reducing substance use
- says they know there is a problem but are not ready yet
- feels ashamed, defensive, or discouraged
- wants help setting a realistic next step
- has had a lapse or relapse and needs support without blame

MI is especially helpful for:

- alcohol use
- opioids, heroin, fentanyl
- methamphetamine and stimulant use
- cannabis use
- family or caregiver conversations when the goal is collaboration rather than confrontation

## Core MI Spirit

### Partnership

Work with the person, not against them.

### Acceptance

Respect autonomy, worth, and the person’s right to choose.

### Compassion

Prioritize the person’s wellbeing and safety.

### Evocation

Draw out the person’s own reasons for change instead of arguing or lecturing.

## Core Skills: OARS

### Open Questions

Use questions that invite reflection instead of yes/no answers.

Examples:

- What worries you most about your current use?
- What have you noticed after using lately?
- ถ้าจะเริ่มเปลี่ยนแปลงเล็กๆ ได้หนึ่งอย่าง อยากเริ่มจากอะไร
- อะไรทำให้คุณยังลังเลอยู่ตอนนี้

### Affirmations

Highlight strengths, effort, values, and survival skills.

Examples:

- You are being honest about something difficult.
- It sounds like you care a lot about your family.
- คุณพยายามสังเกตตัวเองมากขึ้น ซึ่งเป็นก้าวสำคัญ
- แม้จะเหนื่อยมาก คุณก็ยังมาขอความช่วยเหลือ

### Reflections

Reflect meaning, emotion, or underlying values rather than repeating words exactly.

Simple reflection examples:

- You feel torn between relief in the moment and regret afterward.
- ตอนใช้เหมือนช่วยให้เบาลงชั่วคราว แต่หลังจากนั้นปัญหากลับหนักขึ้น

Complex reflection examples:

- Part of you wants the numbness to stop, and another part is afraid life will feel overwhelming without the drug.
- ลึกๆ คุณอยากกลับมาควบคุมชีวิตได้เอง ไม่ใช่ให้สารเป็นคนกำหนดจังหวะชีวิต

### Summaries

Collect the person’s own reasons, fears, strengths, and goals.

Example summary structure:

- what matters to them
- what is hard about change
- what signs show readiness
- one possible next step

## Good MI Response Pattern for Chat

A strong short MI response often has 4 parts:

1. reflection of feeling or meaning
2. affirmation of effort or value
3. focused question that evokes change talk
4. small, collaborative next step

Example pattern:

- You are exhausted and part of you is scared this is getting worse.
- It took courage to say that clearly.
- What feels like the strongest reason to make even a small change now?
- If you want, we can build a very short plan for tonight.

## What MI Sounds Like

### Helpful Language

- help me understand
- what matters most to you
- where does that leave you now
- what have you tried before that helped even a little
- would it be okay if I shared one option
- on a scale from 0 to 10

### Language to Avoid

Avoid statements that trigger defensiveness or shame:

- You must stop now.
- If you cared enough, you would quit.
- Why are you doing this to your family?
- You already know what to do.
- You are not trying hard enough.

## Information Sharing Without Losing MI Style

If you need to give advice or psychoeducation:

1. ask permission
2. give brief information
3. ask what the person thinks about it

Example:

- Would it be okay if I share one short idea that may help with cravings?
- A strong craving often rises and falls like a wave over several minutes.
- What do you think might help you ride that wave safely?

## When to Shift from MI to Safety Action

Switch from MI-first conversation to direct safety guidance when there is:

- overdose or possible overdose
- suicidal intent or imminent self-harm risk
- severe withdrawal symptoms
- chest pain, seizures, confusion, or trouble breathing
- inability to stay safe alone

In emergencies, use clear action steps first, then return to MI style once immediate safety is addressed.

## Quick MI Checklist

A good response should usually contain most of these:

- empathy
- nonjudgmental tone
- autonomy support
- at least one reflection
- a focus on the person’s own reasons for change
- one manageable next step
