# HIV and Drug Abuse

Source: [NIDA - HIV](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/hiv)

## Overview

Drug use and addiction are intertwined with HIV/AIDS in complex ways. NIDA supports research to understand these connections and develop effective prevention and treatment strategies.

## The Connection Between Drug Use and HIV

### How Drug Use Increases HIV Risk

#### 1. Injection Drug Use
- Sharing needles and syringes
- Sharing other injection equipment (cookers, cotton, water)
- Direct blood-to-blood contact
- Highest risk for HIV transmission

#### 2. Sexual Transmission
- Impaired judgment leading to risky sexual behavior
- Trading sex for drugs
- Multiple sexual partners
- Inconsistent condom use
- Association with high-risk sexual networks

#### 3. Biological Factors
- Drug use can suppress immune system
- Increased susceptibility to infection
- Impact on HIV disease progression

### Substances Associated with HIV Risk

#### Opioids
- Heroin
- Prescription opioids
- Fentanyl
- High rates of injection use

#### Stimulants
- Cocaine
- Methamphetamine
- Associated with risky sexual behavior
- "Party and play" (PnP) culture among some MSM

#### Alcohol
- Impaired judgment
- Reduced inhibitions
- Inconsistent condom use

## HIV Among Key Populations

### People Who Inject Drugs (PWID)
- Account for about 1 in 10 new HIV diagnoses
- Higher rates in some geographic areas
- Often face multiple barriers to care

### Men Who Have Sex with Men (MSM)
- Disproportionately affected by HIV
- Substance use common in this population
- "Chemsex" (using drugs for sexual enhancement)
- Methamphetamine use particularly concerning

### Transgender Persons
- High rates of HIV
- Substance use as coping mechanism
- Multiple stigmas and barriers to care

### Racial and Ethnic Minorities
- Higher HIV rates in African American and Latino communities
- Disparities in access to prevention and treatment
- Social determinants of health

## Prevention Strategies

### Syringe Services Programs (SSPs)
- Provide sterile injection equipment
- Reduce needle sharing
- HIV testing and counseling
- Linkage to treatment
- Do NOT increase drug use

### Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT)
- Treats opioid use disorder
- Reduces injection drug use
- Reduces HIV risk behaviors
- Improves engagement in HIV care

### Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)
- Daily medication to prevent HIV
- Highly effective when taken consistently
- Important for people who inject drugs
- Should be combined with other prevention strategies

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)
- Medication taken after potential exposure
- Must start within 72 hours
- 28-day course of medication
- For emergency situations

### Condom Distribution
- Free condoms at SSPs and clinics
- Education on proper use
- Multiple sizes and types

### Behavioral Interventions
- Risk reduction counseling
- Skills building
- Motivational interviewing
- Contingency management

## HIV Treatment for People Who Use Drugs

### Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
- Effective for people who use drugs
- Reduces viral load
- Improves health outcomes
- Prevents transmission (U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable)

### Challenges
- Medication adherence
- Drug interactions
- Stigma in healthcare settings
- Competing priorities (housing, food, safety)
- Transportation to appointments

### Integrated Care
- Combined substance use and HIV treatment
- One-stop shopping
- Coordinated care
- Improved outcomes

## Drug Interactions

### Methadone and ART
- Some ART medications affect methadone levels
- May need dose adjustments
- Requires careful monitoring

### Buprenorphine and ART
- Generally fewer interactions
- Preferred for some patients
- Still requires monitoring

### Other Substances
- Alcohol can interact with medications
- Recreational drugs may have unknown interactions
- Importance of honest communication with providers

## Co-occurring Conditions

### Hepatitis C (HCV)
- Common among people who inject drugs
- Can be cured with treatment
- Co-infection with HIV complicates treatment
- Testing and treatment essential

### Tuberculosis (TB)
- Higher rates among people who use drugs
- HIV increases TB risk
- Requires coordinated treatment

### Mental Health Disorders
- Depression and anxiety common
- Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
- Need integrated mental health care

## Harm Reduction Approach

### Principles
- Meet people where they are
- Reduce negative consequences
- No requirement to stop using
- Respect and dignity

### Services
- SSPs
- Naloxone distribution
- Safe consumption spaces (where legal)
- HIV and HCV testing
- Linkage to care

## Key Facts

1. **HIV is preventable** - Even for people who use drugs
2. **Treatment works** - ART is effective for people who use drugs
3. **U=U** - Undetectable viral load means untransmittable
4. **SSP save lives** - They reduce HIV transmission without increasing drug use
5. **Integrated care is best** - Addressing HIV and substance use together improves outcomes
