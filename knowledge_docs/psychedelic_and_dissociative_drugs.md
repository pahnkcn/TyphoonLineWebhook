# Psychedelic and Dissociative Drugs

Source: [NIDA - Psychedelic and Dissociative Drugs](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/psychedelic-dissociative-drugs)

## Overview

Psychedelic and dissociative drugs are substances that alter perception, mood, and cognitive processes. While some have been used for centuries in religious and cultural contexts, others are synthetic compounds created in laboratories.

## Categories

### 1. Psychedelics (Classic Hallucinogens)

#### How They Work
- Primarily affect serotonin receptors (5-HT2A)
- Alter perception, mood, and cognition
- Can cause hallucinations
- Effects vary by substance, dose, and individual

#### Common Psychedelics

##### LSD (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide)
**Also called:** Acid, Blotter, Dots, Mellow Yellow

- **Source:** Synthesized from ergot fungus
- **Potency:** Very potent (microgram doses)
- **Duration:** 8-12 hours
- **Effects:** Visual hallucinations, altered time perception, spiritual experiences, synesthesia
- **Risks:** Psychological distress, accidents, HPPD (persistent hallucinations)

##### Psilocybin (Magic Mushrooms)
**Also called:** Shrooms, Mushrooms, Magic Mushrooms

- **Source:** Certain mushroom species (Psilocybe)
- **Duration:** 4-6 hours
- **Effects:** Similar to LSD but shorter duration, euphoria, altered perception, mystical experiences
- **Risks:** Psychological distress, misidentification of toxic mushrooms

##### Mescaline (Peyote)
**Also called:** Buttons, Cactus, Mesc

- **Source:** Peyote cactus, San Pedro cactus
- **Duration:** 10-12 hours
- **Effects:** Visual hallucinations, altered consciousness, nausea
- **Legal status:** Peyote legal for Native American religious use
- **Risks:** Nausea, vomiting, psychological distress

##### DMT (Dimethyltryptamine)
**Also called:** Businessman's Trip, Dimitri

- **Source:** Naturally occurring in some plants; can be synthesized
- **Duration:** Very short (30-45 minutes when smoked)
- **Effects:** Intense visual hallucinations, out-of-body experiences, spiritual encounters
- **Forms:** Smoked, brewed as ayahuasca (with MAOI)
- **Risks:** Psychological distress, dangerous behavior

### 2. Dissociative Drugs

#### How They Work
- Block NMDA glutamate receptors
- Create feelings of detachment
- Dissociation from self and environment

#### Common Dissociatives

##### Ketamine
**Also called:** K, Special K, Cat Valium, Kit Kat

- **Medical use:** Anesthetic (Schedule III)
- **Duration:** 1-2 hours
- **Effects:** Dissociation, anesthesia, pain relief, hallucinations at higher doses
- **Medical potential:** Treatment-resistant depression (esketamine/Spravato® FDA-approved)
- **Risks:** Bladder damage (ketamine bladder), memory loss, addiction, "K-hole" (profound dissociation)

##### PCP (Phencyclidine)
**Also called:** Angel Dust, Rocket Fuel, Supergrass

- **History:** Developed as anesthetic (discontinued due to side effects)
- **Duration:** 4-6 hours
- **Effects:** Dissociation, euphoria, aggression, hallucinations
- **Risks:** Violent behavior, psychosis, seizures, coma, death, suicide risk

##### DXM (Dextromethorphan)
**Also called:** Robo, Triple C, Skittles

- **Source:** Over-the-counter cough suppressant
- **Duration:** 3-6 hours
- **Effects:** Dissociation, euphoria, hallucinations at high doses
- **Risks:** Confusion, nausea, increased heart rate, brain damage at very high doses

##### Nitrous Oxide
**Also called:** Whippets, Laughing Gas, Hippie Crack

- **Medical use:** Dental anesthetic
- **Duration:** Very brief (minutes)
- **Effects:** Euphoria, dissociation, laughter, sound distortion
- **Risks:** Oxygen deprivation (with heavy use), B12 deficiency, nerve damage, "sudden sniffing death"

### 3. Atypical Psychedelics

##### MDMA (Ecstasy/Molly)
**Also called:** E, X, Molly, Adam

- **Classification:** Stimulant with psychedelic properties
- **Duration:** 3-6 hours
- **Effects:** Euphoria, emotional openness, increased empathy, enhanced sensory perception
- **Medical potential:** PTSD treatment (in clinical trials)
- **Risks:** Hyperthermia, dehydration, heart problems, depression, memory problems

##### Salvia Divinorum
**Also called:** Diviner's Sage, Maria Pastora

- **Source:** Mexican mint plant
- **Duration:** Very short (5-30 minutes)
- **Effects:** Intense but brief hallucinations, dissociation
- **Legal status:** Varies by state
- **Risks:** Loss of coordination, dangerous behavior

##### Ibogaine
**Source:** African shrub (Tabernanthe iboga)

- **Duration:** 24-36 hours
- **Effects:** Intense hallucinations, ataxia
- **Claimed use:** Addiction interruption (not FDA-approved)
- **Risks:** Cardiac toxicity, death

## Effects of Psychedelic and Dissociative Drugs

### Short-term Effects

#### Physical Effects
- Increased heart rate and blood pressure
- Dilated pupils
- Nausea and vomiting
- Loss of appetite
- Sleep disturbances
- Uncoordinated movements
- Increased body temperature

#### Psychological Effects
- Hallucinations (visual, auditory, tactile)
- Altered perception of time and space
- Intense emotions (positive and negative)
- Spiritual or mystical experiences
- Synesthesia (blending of senses)
- Ego dissolution (loss of sense of self)
- Anxiety or panic ("bad trip")

### Long-term Effects

#### Potential Risks
- **Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD)**
  - Flashbacks or persistent visual disturbances
  - Can last months or years
  - More common with LSD

- **Persistent Psychosis**
  - Visual disturbances
  - Disorganized thinking
  - Paranoia
  - Mood changes
  - Rare but serious

- **Psychological Dependence**
  - Craving for experiences
  - Not typically physically addictive

#### Potential Benefits (Research Areas)
- Treatment-resistant depression
- PTSD
- End-of-life anxiety
- Addiction treatment
- **Note:** Research ongoing; not yet standard treatments

## Therapeutic Potential

### Current Research

#### Psilocybin
- Depression (treatment-resistant)
- Anxiety in terminal illness
- Alcohol use disorder
- Smoking cessation

#### MDMA
- PTSD (Phase 3 clinical trials)
- Social anxiety in autism

#### Ketamine
- Treatment-resistant depression (FDA-approved as esketamine)
- Suicidal ideation
- Chronic pain

#### LSD
- Anxiety in terminal illness
- Depression
- Cluster headaches

### Important Considerations
- Research is in controlled clinical settings
- Not the same as recreational use
- Professional guidance essential
- Screening for contraindications
- Integration therapy important

## Risks and Safety

### Set and Setting
- **Set:** User's mindset, expectations, mental state
- **Setting:** Physical and social environment
- Both significantly influence experience

### Who Should NOT Use Psychedelics
- Personal or family history of psychotic disorders
- Bipolar disorder
- Severe anxiety disorders
- Cardiovascular conditions
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- Those taking certain medications (MAOIs, SSRIs)

### Harm Reduction
- Never use alone
- Have a sober sitter
- Start with low dose
- Choose safe environment
- Avoid mixing with other substances
- Know the source
- Be prepared for difficult experiences

### Managing "Bad Trips"
- Remind person effects are temporary
- Move to quiet, safe space
- Reassure and calm
- Avoid physical restraint
- Seek medical help if necessary

## Legal Status

### Controlled Substances
- Most psychedelics are Schedule I (high abuse potential, no accepted medical use)
- Ketamine is Schedule III
- Some exceptions for religious use (peyote for Native Americans)
- Legal status changing in some jurisdictions (decriminalization, therapeutic use)

### Changing Landscape
- Oregon legalized psilocybin for therapeutic use
- Some cities decriminalized possession
- FDA breakthrough therapy designation for MDMA and psilocybin
- Research expanding

## Historical and Cultural Context

### Traditional Use
- Peyote in Native American Church
- Ayahuasca in Amazonian cultures
- Psilocybin mushrooms in Mesoamerican rituals
- Ibogaine in African Bwiti religion

### Modern Era
- 1950s-60s: LSD research and recreational use
- 1970: Controlled Substances Act (most psychedelics banned)
- 1990s-present: Renewed research interest
- 2000s: Johns Hopkins psilocybin studies
- 2010s-present: "Psychedelic renaissance"

## Research Challenges

### Regulatory Barriers
- Schedule I classification limits research
- Difficult to obtain and store substances
- Extensive approval processes

### Methodological Challenges
- Blinding difficult (active placebo)
- Expectation effects strong
- Individual variability high
- Defining "therapeutic" experiences

### Safety Concerns
- Psychological risks
- Long-term effects unknown
- Contraindications
- Integration needs
